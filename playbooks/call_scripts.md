# Calls

You are the first person the site reaches. Use the script. Do not freelance a reset procedure.

## Before you speak

Confirm the asset. A ticket can name the wrong robot. Check which asset actually changed. If telemetry agrees with the ticket, say that and still confirm it out loud.

Check the last buckets: belt state, e-stop, fault code, air, picks, and whether the sibling robots are still picking.

## Robot not picking

1. Say who you are and which asset you think they are standing at.
2. Ask them to stay clear of the arm and read the HMI banner.
3. If the banner is an e-stop, ask whether the area is clear. They may reset only by the card posted on that robot. You do not invent extra steps. You do not tell them to wedge the button, jumper a circuit, or "just clear the fault in software."
4. If the reset works, ask them to watch one minute of picks and call you back if it stops again. Write "reset reported by the site, not yet confirmed in telemetry" until a new bucket shows picks.
5. If it does not reset, or the e-stop is still set in the latest bucket and the call is still open, leave the robot paused, route Field Service, and do not promise it for a tour.
6. If air is low or the sibling on the same belt is healthy, say you are sending hardware. Do not call it a software bug on the phone.

## What you may not do

- Do not tell them the problem is fixed if the last bucket is still in fault.
- Do not give a root cause you have not checked. "The e-stop is latched" is an observation. "Someone hit it" is a guess.
- Do not blame the caller.
- Internal team names stay out of the customer email unless they ask who is coming.

## Opening line you can practice

"Thanks for holding. This is [name] at Northline. I have Cedar fiber robot 1, RBT-CDR-01. Our side shows the belt moving and an e-stop since 6:45 AM Pacific. Please stay clear of the arm and read me the banner before anyone resets it."
